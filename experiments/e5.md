## Findings

- **laguna at 128k is not better than at 64k.** 26/36 against 25/36 overall. On the six screening tasks, the totals
  pooled over the E3/E4 screens and E5 are 28/36 at 128k against 25/36 at 64k: a small edge for 128k, still within
  noise for a sample this size. 128k spills 10% of the model off the GPU and peaks at 7.1 GiB RAM and 1.5 GiB swap
  (of 7.4 GiB RAM and 8 GiB swap). 64k fits fully, peaks at 6.3 GiB and is as fast end to end (33.0 against 36.3
  solved per 8 h).
- **qwen3-coder is 3.4x laguna's throughput at 0.58 against 0.69-0.72 first-time success.** It solves the easy
  tasks in about 2 minutes. It loses most on 002-damage-signal and 008-hud-health (1/3 each, against 2-3/3 for
  laguna).
- **Three of qwen's 15 failures were tool calls Ollama didn't parse.** In 001 r1, 002 r1 and 003 r2 the model wrote
  a correct `<function=...>...</function></tool_call>` without the opening `<tool_call>` tag. Ollama's
  `qwen3-coder` parser then returned the call as text, and OpenCode ended the attempt with no tool used (20-25 s,
  under 100 tokens). The same pattern appears in most earlier Ollama qwen runs (about 1 in 18 attempts). It did not
  happen in any of the 36 llama.cpp E2 attempts, so llama.cpp's parser seems to accept the missing tag.
- **On llama.cpp Vulkan, qwen3-coder passes more often: 49/72 (0.68) against 42/72 (0.58) on Ollama** over two
  finalists runs each (25 and 24 against 21 and 21). The gains are on 001, 002, 003 and 012, where Ollama's early
  quits fell. Across the two Ollama runs 4 of 72 attempts ended at once on an unparsed call; none of the 72
  Vulkan attempts did. Generation is about 9% slower (24.4-24.6 against 26.7-26.9 tok/s).
- **Throughput depends on whether the model stops.** In the Vulkan repeat, both 003-score-combo attempts fixed the
  bug early and then looped for 38 minutes (about 140 tool calls each, re-running the tests and rewriting the todo
  list) before finishing just inside the 45-minute limit. Both passed, but those two attempts take the run from
  about 1.5 to 2.8 agent hours and its solved per 8 h from about 125 to 68.1. Pooled, Vulkan solves 87.5 per 8 h
  (49 in 4.48 h) against 108.0 for Ollama (42 in 3.11 h). Without the two loops, Vulkan would lead on both. Ollama
  had no loops this long in 72 attempts; whether llama.cpp makes them likelier is not settled by two attempts on
  one task.
- **Two tasks are out of reach for both models.** 010-wave-spawner was 0/18 across all six E5 runs (best 6/7 hidden
  tests) and 005-inventory-pickup 1/18. 011-status-effects is 3/18.
- **Recommendation:**
  - *Overnight queue:* qwen3-coder:30b Q4_K_M, 32k, f16 KV with OpenCode, on llama.cpp Vulkan b12191
    (`experiments/e5-vk-qwen-ctx32k/`) for the higher pass rate. A shorter per-attempt limit (for example 15 minutes;
    every other passing qwen attempt finished within 6 minutes) would bound the looping case and recover the
    throughput. Ollama 0.34.4 is the fallback with the same model, at about 10 fewer passes per 100 attempts.
  - *First-time success:* laguna-xs-2.1 Q4_K_M at 64k on Ollama 0.34.4 (llama.cpp was slower for laguna in E2). 128k gives no measurable gain and leaves under 0.5 GiB of RAM
    free at peak.
- **Proposed next:**
  - Re-run qwen on Vulkan with a 15-minute limit to measure throughput with the loops cut off.
  - Find why llama-server generates about 12% slower than Ollama on the same GGUF (flash attention, batch sizes).
  - Cap Godot's memory during scoring (for example `systemd-run --user -p MemoryMax=2G`), so a runaway in agent
    code counts as a failed attempt and cannot push the model server out of memory.
